# Session Log — Arrakis, 2031-04-02

A Chalice Run crew of three arrives at Farrukh's den with six hours before a water run needs to leave. A rival forecaster elsewhere in the fringe markets has already told them, flatly, that the window is clear for the next two days.

Farrukh pulls the same data the rival used, plus two more signals he didn't cite, and gives them a storm window of "probably clear for 30 hours, maybe 50, I wouldn't bet the crew's lives past 40." She names her confidence interval unprompted, the way she always does, and charges the standard 40 water-credit whether they like the answer or not.

The crew argues. One member wants the rival's cleaner answer because a run planned around "probably 30-50 hours" is logistically worse than one planned around a flat "two days." Another points out that the rival has been wrong before and never says so afterward — there's no record of the rival's track record because the rival doesn't keep one publicly.

They take Farrukh's forecast and plan for the 30-hour floor. The run finishes in 34 hours. Whether that counts as her forecast being right is, per the session's own framing, exactly the kind of question the line exists to leave open — a tighter storm arrived at hour 38, which would have caught them under the rival's flat two-day estimate.
