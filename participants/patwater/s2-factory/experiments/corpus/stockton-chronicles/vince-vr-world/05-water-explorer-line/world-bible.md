# Water Explorer Line — World Bible

## What this line is

Inside The Interchange, the Water Explorer is presented as a civic-simulation tool rather than a game with a narrative arc. Members — and, notionally, real water-sector professionals who visit the co-op — use it to explore how a household's or small business's water bill might look under different future scenarios. This line is structurally different from the other four: it is not fiction members step out of at the end of a session, and its Conductor corpus is held to the stricter sourcing standard documented in `real-data-sources.md`.

## Real foundation

The tool's rate-standardization approach and current real figures come from `whatwatercosts.org`'s published methodology and rate data (see `real-data-sources.md` for full citations, retrieval dates, and the credit to Maxwell, the tool's real maintainer). Every number the Conductor states as a *current* fact in this line must trace to that file. Every number it states as a *future* projection must be marked speculative and disclaimed, per the rule below.

## The four-archetype scenario structure

This line uses the same four-archetype framework already established elsewhere for California 2042 scenario work — Transformation, Growth, Constraint, Collapse — applied specifically to water affordability, rather than inventing a new taxonomy. For each archetype, the tool projects how three things might plausibly evolve together: rate structure (tiered pricing, fixed vs. volumetric charges, Prop 218 constraints on how a charge can be justified), supply mix (recycled water, desalination, imported-supply reliability), and affordability programs (low-income rate assistance, payment plans).

- **Transformation** — supply mix diversifies faster than demand grows; rate structures shift toward higher fixed charges to fund the new infrastructure, offset by expanded affordability programs.
- **Growth** — demand grows roughly in step with supply investment; rates rise steadily but predictably, tracking population and infrastructure-maintenance costs.
- **Constraint** — supply growth lags demand and climate volatility; volumetric tiers steepen sharply to manage peak demand, and affordability programs come under the most fiscal pressure of the four archetypes.
- **Collapse** — a scenario where affordability programs fail to keep pace with rate increases at all, and a meaningful share of households face service disruption risk.

**[speculative: true]** Illustrative example only, not a forecast: under a Constraint-archetype projection for a Stockton-area household, Tier 2 volumetric charges (currently $3.32/unit per `real-data-sources.md`) might plausibly rise faster than Tier 1 charges over a 10–15 year horizon as agencies lean harder on tiered pricing to manage peak demand, while base service charges rise more slowly. This is illustrative scenario modeling only. It is not a rate forecast, and no one should use it to inform an actual financial decision.

## Rate analyst NPC role

A lighter-touch NPC than the other four lines — see `npc-rate-analyst.md` — narrates the tool Socratically for a visiting member. This line is closer to a facilitated calculator than a story.
