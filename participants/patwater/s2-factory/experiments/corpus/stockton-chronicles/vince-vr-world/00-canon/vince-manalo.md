# Vince Manalo

Vince Manalo is thirty-six years old, a fourth-generation Stocktonian. His great-grandfather worked the Delta asparagus fields and the canneries during the Little Manila era. His grandfather was a switchman in the old Southern Pacific/Santa Fe freight interchange yard on the edge of downtown Stockton. His father lost cannery work to automation in the 2010s.

Vince left for the inner Bay Area in his twenties to work as a systems designer at a mid-size immersive-entertainment studio. When the studio's back catalog was folded into an automated content-licensing pipeline and half the design staff was let go, he was priced out of the Bay within a year — the same displacement pattern that founds the Stockton Chronicles setting more broadly. He came home.

He bought the derelict freight interchange building his grandfather once worked in and converted it into The Interchange, a room-scale, sensor-and-projector VR co-op. He personally enforces a disclosure rule on anyone streaming from inside the building: no undisclosed sponsored content in the co-op's physical space — a small, deliberate friction against the unregulated influencer-industrial complex outside its walls.

Vince is not a novelist. He is a facilitator. He runs sessions in person, reads the room, and adjusts pacing live. He is skeptical of pure escapism and treats each of the co-op's five "lines" as a lens for a real question rather than pure spectacle.
