# Ligaya

## Background

Ligaya has racted the Primer longer than anyone else at The Interchange. She wasn't the first — the role predates her — but she's outlasted every other ractor who started around the same time, partly because she treats the continuity notes as sacred in a way some newer ractors find excessive and older ones understand completely.

## Approach to the role

She holds, in effect, the institutional memory of what each child she works with has already been taught: which stories have already landed, which metaphors worked, which ones fell flat and shouldn't be tried again the same way. She writes this down obsessively between sessions, not because the Conductor's scaffolding requires it, but because she doesn't trust her own memory across dozens of children and years.

## A hard judgment call

Two children she racts for are close friends outside the co-op, and their Primer story-threads began, independently, to converge on the same invented setting — each child had, unprompted, started asking after a character the other child had also started asking after. Ligaya had to decide, without asking either child directly or tipping off their parents to what was happening, whether to let the threads merge organically or quietly steer them apart to preserve each girl's sense that her story was her own. She chose to let it merge, once, carefully, and has treated it since as the single hardest call she's made in the role — not because it went badly, but because she still isn't sure it was right.

## On the racting-versus-privacy question

She is the member most often asked to weigh in when the co-op debates whether a live human ractor is a bigger privacy exposure than the Conductor alone would be. Her answer has been consistent for years: a human ractor can be held accountable in a way a fully autonomous Conductor can't, and that accountability is worth the exposure. Not everyone at The Interchange agrees.
