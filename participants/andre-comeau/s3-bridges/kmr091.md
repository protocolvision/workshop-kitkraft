## What my agent needed from the target

`fair-price-justification` can only cite what is already sitting in its own
`sources/register.csv` — it has no built-in way to go check whether a citation is
still current at its real source. That register has one row exactly in that state:
`TBS-CPN-2007-4` (Contracting Policy Notice 2007-4), status
`UNVERIFIED-read-at-source-before-use`, and `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` #4 asks the same
thing in prose: *"'Fair and reasonable' wording: not found in the [current]
Directive. Read Contracting Policy Notice 2007-4 at source and confirm whether the
phrase survives in any current instrument."* That is the one thing my kit cannot
do alone that kmr091's — "a dated, sourced read on what's changed in a policy
area" — is built to do. Task: answer that open question for real.

## What it found on its own

Read `kmr091/s2-factory/README.md`, then `SKILL.md`, then `examples/`, cold — no
question put to kmr091. Followed the kit's own four steps, supplying the inputs

policy area = Canadian federal procurement policy; sub-topic = whether "fair and
reasonable price" wording survives in a current instrument; baseline = the
register's own retrieval date, 2026-09-21. Searched real public sources (not
synthetic), weighed the government source over commentary, and drafted the
briefing the kit's step 4 asks for:

> **Policy developments briefing — "fair and reasonable price" wording, Canadian
> federal procurement — since 2026-09-21**
>
> - **Contracting Policy Notice 2007-4** (canada.ca, retrieved 2026-09-22) — the
>   phrase is there: Annex A, Question 6, "Explain why the price is fair and
>   reasonable, describe how price support was obtained, and summarize
>   negotiations," tied to the "only one person capable of performing the work"
>   non-competitive exception. No rescission notice on the page. Last amended
>   2019-06-10 "to reflect changes to the Government Contracts Regulations."
> - **Directive on the Management of Procurement** (tbs-sct.canada.ca, retrieved
>   2026-09-22; effective 2021-05-13, amended through 2026-04-30) — full body text
>   (sections 1–7) read directly. The phrase **does not appear anywhere in it**.
>   Its actual language is "fair, open and transparent" (3.2.6, 4.4.2) or
>   "fairness, openness and transparency" (4.3.1, 5.4.1) — a broader
>   process-fairness phrase, not a price-specific one. This matches what the
>   register already has verified at 4.3.1 (paraphrased there as exactly that).
> - **The gap neither source states plainly**: CPN 2007-4 supplemented the old
>   "Contracting Policy," which this Directive explicitly replaced on
>   2021-05-13. The Directive's own text never restates or cross-references the
>   Notice, and the Notice's own page never says whether it was folded in,
>   carried forward, or superseded along with the policy it belonged to.
> - **Not checked**: the Directive's Appendices (A–F) are separate pages and
>   were not read; a PSPC-specific non-competitive justification template (which
>   may have replaced CPN 2007-4's Annex A form directly) was not searched for.
>   Coverage on those two points is thin — said plainly, per the kit's own rule.
>
> **Implications for real-time policy decisions**: `TBS-CPN-2007-4` can move from
> `UNVERIFIED` to verified-on-page — the phrase is real, current, and unrescinded
> at that URL. But it should carry a new caveat rather than close cleanly: its
> authority rests on a Notice tied to a superseded parent policy, not on the
> Directive that now actually governs procurement. Treat "fair and reasonable" as
> well-attested historically, not as textually anchored in the instrument in
> force today, and confirm with procurement-policy staff whether the Notice is
> still treated as live before the template cites it as a hard requirement — this
> is a first take, not a conclusion, exactly as the target kit's own rule requires.

## Did it work

Partly, and on purpose. The mechanism worked with no one from kmr091's side in
the room: the README + SKILL.md were enough to run the kit correctly end to end
(independence held). It produced a real, checkable finding, not a placeholder.
But the *output* is deliberately partial, not a closed answer — which is the
correct outcome for a kit whose own constraint is "a view is a first take, not a
conclusion," bridged into a kit whose own constraint is "if a needed provision is
not in the register, write 'needs lookup,' never guess." Both sides refused to
overclaim; the bridge is where that showed up.

## What a bridge turned out to be

A bridge held here for a narrower reason than I expected going in: not because
the two kits share a data shape (they do not — one produces a drafted document,
the other a briefing), but because they share the same refusal. `kmr091`'s rule
against presenting a view as a conclusion and `fair-price-justification`'s rule
against citing anything not in the register turned out to be the same discipline
wearing two names, and that shared discipline is what let one kit's output slot
directly into the other's open question without anyone smoothing over the edges.
Where a bridge would have broken is if either kit had been willing to guess to
close the gap — neither was, so the handoff was a real update to a real register
row, with its own honestly-stated limit still attached, rather than a tidier
answer than the evidence supports.
