## term: nature
status: CONTESTED

### position: RUSKIN
holds: the artist's job is to receive what is there, without selection.
ex: "rejecting nothing, selecting nothing, and scorning nothing" — Modern Painters I, 1843
anti-ex: "a coxcomb ask two hundred guineas for flinging a pot of paint in the public's face" — Fors Clavigera 79, 1877
refuses: reject a work whose arrangement is the artist's own rather than observed — where you cannot find the thing that was looked at.

### position: WHISTLER
holds: nature supplies material, not composition; the artist selects.
ex: "the artist is born to pick, and choose, and group with science, these elements" — Ten O'Clock, 1885
anti-ex: "Nature is very rarely right, to such an extent even, that it might almost be said that Nature is usually wrong" — Ten O'Clock, 1885
refuses: reject a work that reproduces nature's own arrangement — where no choice was made.

## term: labour
status: CONTESTED

### position: RUSKIN
holds: value is the visible work put in; price tracks labour.
ex: "walk with her laboriously and trustingly" — Modern Painters I, 1843
anti-ex: "two hundred guineas for flinging a pot of paint" — Fors Clavigera 79, 1877
refuses: reject a work whose making cannot be read off its surface.

### position: WHISTLER
holds: value is accumulated judgement, which leaves no trace of effort.
ex: "I ask it for the knowledge of a lifetime" — Whistler v. Ruskin, 1878
anti-ex: "The labour of two days is that for which you ask two hundred guineas?" — Attorney-General, Whistler v. Ruskin, 1878, rejected by the answer
refuses: reject a work that displays its effort in order to be valued for it.
