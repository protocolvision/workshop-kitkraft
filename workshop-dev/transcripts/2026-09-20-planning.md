# 2026-09-20 — Facilitator planning call, eve of the workshop

Rafa and Sachin. Half theory, half final preparation.

## Refining the kit definition: how protocolized was the world the car arrived in?

The car analogy had been carrying a lot of weight in the session-1 material, and the
call opened by testing it.

The refinement: early cars arrived into a **very low-protocolization environment**.
There were no roads worth the name, no interstate highways, no traffic law, no
traffic authorities. AI, by contrast, arrives into a world already heavily
protocolized — Git and GitHub are the trivial examples, and the whole stack of
frameworks and working practices sits underneath them. On that reading the closer
literary analogy is not the pioneer tinkerer but J. G. Ballard's *Crash* and
*Concrete Island*: working something out inside an environment that is already
completely built up.

A distinction was drawn between protocolization and a *skill* environment. The people
tinkering with early car kits already had experience working with machines, so there
was an environment in which the relevant skills existed — but that is not the same
thing as an environment governed by protocols. The evidence offered for the broader
claim was the Protocol Institute's graph showing use of the word "protocol" climbing
after the Second World War.

**The counter-argument**, and the most interesting exchange of the call: is
"protocolized" an objective property or a judgement we can only make from inside our
own moment? Would a farmer of that period have experienced their environment as
protocolized? It may be that we are in a comparatively *non*-protocolized environment
and cannot see it — that in fifty years everything will look hyperprotocolized by
comparison, in the way a farmer could not have conceptualised an automated farm.

Neither position was abandoned. The graph is a reasonable rubric for protocolization
increasing over time; it does not settle whether the current level is high or low in
absolute terms.

Two extensions came out of it. First, AI is a special case because it is **oozy** and
because it protocolizes environments itself — so the dynamics may not mirror the car
at all. Second, this is an oscillation between **smooth and striated**: AI makes some
already-striated fields smoother and oozier, and does the reverse elsewhere.

The practical payoff: maturity levels make sense precisely in an environment that
already has many protocols, where the problem is managing the introduction of new
ones. That ties the theoretical argument back to the AI CMM.

## Facilitation

The split agreed for session 1: Sachin opens, Rafa covers the Protocol Institute and
the logistics, Sachin takes the kit theory, then hands back to Rafa for the
exercises. Rule of thumb — theory questions go to Sachin, logistics questions to
Rafa. Session 2 was expected to be more of a back-and-forth, being lighter on theory.

## What to include, and what to hold back

The failure-modes and success-patterns material from the AI CMM had not been worked
into the workshop. The judgement was that it probably belongs in a second workshop
rather than this one, and might be too much for a first session — but that the deck
would be kept to hand and pulled up if a moment arose for it.

More generally, the agenda was to be held loosely. If the room wants to go toward
factories, or away from them, or toward a new kit idea, that is a legitimate
direction. Additional AI CMM material was available to pull in if sessions ran short.

## Exercise timing

The inventory exercise was sized at roughly ten minutes, with a second exercise of
similar length in which participants read each other's kits to get a feel for how
other people actually use AI. The expectation was that this would move quickly
because the participants' assistants are doing the work rather than the participants.

The ambition for the inventory was more than one kit each — ideally around five. One
is acceptable. Where a participant had listed only a couple, the intent was to push
them to find more of what is already wired into their assistant.

## Signs it was working

Two participants had already pushed inventories to the repo before the workshop
began, one filled in and one scaffolded. That was taken as evidence that the setup
instructions worked end to end without a facilitator present, which was the thing
most at risk.
